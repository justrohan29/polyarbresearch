# Quantitative Arbitrage: Bridging Binary Prediction Markets and Linear Option Spreads
**An Architectural Overview of the Polymarket/Deribit Delta Engine**

## 1. Abstract & Thesis
This research investigates the structural inefficiencies between Decentralized Binary Prediction Markets (Polymarket) and Centralized Linear Options Exchanges (Deribit). By identifying mispricings between a binary "step-function" payout and a linear option spread, we isolate mathematical arbitrages. We resolve the inherent "Pin Risk" via Implied Volatility probability cones and execute trades that are fully net of exchange macro-fees, securing a positive Annualized Percentage Yield (APY) regardless of the underlying asset's direction.

## 2. The Synthetic Digital Spread (Legging & Construction)
Polymarket offers a pure binary (digital) option that settles at exactly $1.00 or $0.00. Because Deribit (and traditional finance) heavily restricts true digital options for retail, we synthetically construct a digital payout using a tight linear vertical spread.

* **Bull Hedge (Betting UP):** We purchase a `PolyNo` contract (short exposure). To perfectly hedge this, we buy a Deribit **Call Spread** (Long $K, Short $K+1000). 
* **Bear Hedge (Betting DOWN):** We purchase a `PolyYes` contract (long exposure). To hedge this, we buy a Deribit **Put Spread** (Long $K+1000, Short $K).

By normalizing the Deribit contract sizes, a $1,000-wide linear spread mathematically replicates a $1.00 payout binary option outside of the strike boundaries. If the combined cost of the `Poly` token and the Deribit spread is less than $1.00, it constitutes a theoretical arbitrage.

## 3. Microstructure Friction: Liquidity and Fee Caps
A theoretical arbitrage is often an illusion generated by exchange microstructures. Our system (`liquidity_scanner.py`) filters these traps:
1. **The Liquidity Mirage:** Order books often show an attractive `Best Ask`, but Level-2 depth reveals the resting size is negligible. Our engines actively parse L2 order book depth to ensure exactly 1.0 BTC of liquidity exists at the target price before flagging an arbitrage.
2. **The 12.5% Fee Cap:** Deribit's option fee structure (`min(0.0003, 0.125 * option_price)`) creates a massive friction on deep out-of-the-money options. Because our synthetic digital spreads rely on cheap OTM options, the 12.5% cap almost universally triggers, heavily compressing the arbitrage margin. Our engines actively integrate this exact fee on both legs to output a true Net-of-Fees yield.

## 4. Time Resolution: The "Engulfing Expiry"
Polymarket daily markets typically resolve at 16:00 UTC (12:00 PM EST). Deribit daily options unconditionally expire at 08:00 UTC. Hedging a same-day Polymarket event with a same-day Deribit option creates an unhedged 8-hour window where catastrophic Gamma blowout can occur. 
* **Solution:** We algorithmically map all Polymarket events to Deribit options expiring on the **following day** (T+1). This "Engulfing Expiry" ensures the Deribit hedge outlives the Polymarket resolution, preserving the risk-free lock.

## 5. Dynamic Risk Modeling: The IV Probability Cone
Because we use a $1,000-wide linear spread to replicate a step-function digital option, we are exposed to "Pin Risk". If the underlying asset expires exactly between our two strikes, Polymarket pays $0, but the Deribit spread pays less than $1.00, resulting in a severe loss.
* **Solution:** We abandoned static threshold modeling (e.g., avoiding strikes within $2,000). Instead, `matrix_scanner.py` streams live Implied Volatility (`mark_iv`) and calculates a **Black-Scholes 1-Standard-Deviation Probability Cone**: `sd_move = btc * (iv / 100) * math.sqrt(TTE)`. If the target strike falls inside this mathematically dynamic Danger Zone, the system refuses to trade it.

## 6. The Institutional Approach: Binary Delta Hedging (`matrix_simulator.py`)
Moving beyond discrete spread execution, our highest-level implementation is the Global Portfolio Delta Simulator. Rather than purchasing wide option spreads (and paying massive bid-ask spreads), we treat the Polymarket binary option as a purely mathematical Greek.
We calculate the **Binary Delta** for an aggregate portfolio of 16 Polymarket events using the Black-Scholes density function. We then execute dynamic delta-hedging using highly liquid, low-fee **BTC-PERPETUAL** futures to maintain a Delta-Neutral portfolio, absorbing the "Gamma Bleed" (rebalancing fees) while securing the arbitrage spread.
